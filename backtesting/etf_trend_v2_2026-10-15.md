## Definitions (pre-registered)

- **Inherited from v1.** Same 16 ETFs (SPY QQQ IWM EFA EEM TLT GLD XLK XLF XLE XLV XLY XLP XLI XLB XLU); IS 2006-01-03 to 2017-12-29, OOS 2018-01-02 to 2026-10-14; signals on Yahoo AdjClose (total return) at close t, fills at the open of t+1 (open converted to the total-return basis); 5 bp per side on every fill including rebalances; daily mark-to-market. Sharpe is annualised from daily returns with rf = 0 (v1 convention, needed for the anchor); the column "Sharpe ex T-bill" uses returns in excess of the same T-bill and is information plus a labeled sensitivity of the tests.
- **Cash.** Idle money earns the T-bill: daily return = (^IRX at the prior close / 100) × (calendar days since the prior bar) / 360, credited before the open. Synthetic bills have no trading cost and no bid/ask, which slightly flatters every strategy that holds cash. Applies to all series, benchmarks included (their idle cash is only the few bp left after entry costs).
- **B1 (primary).** v1 B: each ETF has a fixed 1/16 sleeve, long when its close > SMA200 at the month-end close, else the sleeve is idle; held sleeves are rebalanced to 1/16. Idle sleeves earn the T-bill.
- **B2.** As B1, but at each month-end the idle sleeves go into IEF if IEF's close > its own SMA200, else T-bill. IEF trades pay 5 bp per side.
- **VT.** SPY exposure = min(1.0, 10% / realized vol), realized vol = stdev of the last 21 daily SPY AdjClose returns × √252, measured at each week's last close (Friday, or Thursday when Friday is a holiday) and traded at the next open; the rest earns the T-bill. No leverage.
- **BL.** Two sub-accounts, 50% B1 and 50% VT, each following its own rule and calendar; at each month-end close both are reset to 50% of total equity at the next open (each account trades back to its current target). Costs are charged per account with no netting between them (conservative).
- **Benchmarks.** SPY buy-and-hold and equal-weight buy-and-hold of the 16 ETFs (bought at each period's first open, never rebalanced — v1 convention), and 60/40 SPY/IEF rebalanced at each month-end (5 bp per trade). Each is bought fresh at the start of IS, of OOS, and of the full period.
- **Seatbelt test.** In BOTH IS and OOS: max drawdown no deeper than 50% of the benchmark's AND Sharpe ≥ the benchmark's. Benchmark: EW buy-and-hold for B1, B2, BL; SPY buy-and-hold for VT.
- **60/40 test.** In BOTH IS and OOS: Sharpe AND CAGR/MaxDD both strictly above the 60/40's.
- **Labeled sensitivities (cannot rescue a failing config).** B1 and B2 with SMA 100 / 150 / 200; VT targets 8% / 10% / 12% × lookbacks 21 / 63 days; VT with monthly rebalance; VT with a 1.5x cap (borrowing at the T-bill, no spread — flatters it); B1 with idle cash in BIL instead of the synthetic bill (OOS only, BIL starts 2007-05-30); every test re-evaluated with Sharpe ex T-bill.
- **Trades.** "Round trips" = v1's definition (a position opened from zero and closed to zero, by entry date; open positions count). "Fills" = every order executed, rebalances included.

## Interpretation (post-hoc)

Written after reading the tables. It does not change any definition, test or verdict.

- **The passes rest mainly on drawdown.** The 60/40 test is passed on CAGR/MaxDD by a wide margin (B1 OOS 0.680 vs 0.432) but on Sharpe by a thin one (0.882 vs 0.835), and the Sharpe side flips for some configs once the T-bill is subtracted. The robust finding is the same as v1's: the trend filter kept B1's drawdowns to less than half of EW buy-and-hold's in 2008, 2020 and 2022 (2022: B1 -7.3% / -8.6% vs EW -16.7% / -23.2%).
- **T-bill interest is a modest correction, not the missing return.** Over the full period the T-bill compounded at 1.7% a year and B1 was idle 27% of the time on average, so the cash effect is a fraction of a point per year (full-period gap closed: 0.4 points). The years it mattered are the high-rate ones: 2023 B1 5.1% vs v1 B 3.3%. The BIL sensitivity shows a real T-bill ETF would have paid less than the synthetic bill in OOS (2.6% vs 2.7%), with B1's OOS CAGR 7.8% instead of 7.9%.
- **Grids.** The SMA100 cells of B1 and B2 pass IS only and decay in OOS (B1 SMA100 OOS CAGR 6.5%); the SMA150 cells have the same verdicts as SMA200. VT is sensitive to its lookback: 63-day cells failing the seatbelt test in OOS: 8% / 63d, 10% / 63d and 12% / 63d (e.g. 10% / 63d OOS MaxDD -22.1%). The 1.5x cap does not improve the OOS Sharpe (0.77 vs 0.86). VT cells that pass both tests in both periods: 8% / 21d; per the rules they do not rescue the pre-registered VT.
- **What this does and does not support.** A month-end SMA200 trend sleeve with cash in T-bills is a defensible drawdown-control sleeve: about 8.1% a year over the full period at a worst drawdown of -13.9%. It does not beat SPY or the EW basket on return, and its edge over 60/40 on risk-adjusted terms is small. BL is the only config that passes everything under both Sharpe conventions, but it is the most tested idea in the set (it combines two other tested configs), and this is the second round of tests on the same data. None of this is a recommendation to change the live PEAD strategy, which this repo cannot correlate against these series.


## Limitations

- **Hindsight ETF universe.** The 16 ETFs were picked today; all survived and stayed liquid. That flatters every strategy and the equal-weight benchmark.
- **Synthetic T-bill with no trading cost.** Idle cash earns ^IRX (a discount-basis yield used as a simple rate, actual/360) with no cost, bid/ask, or lag. It is a slight overstatement of what a real T-bill ETF or sweep would have paid; the BIL sensitivity shows the size of the gap in OOS.
- **Start dates.** IEF has data from 2004 (checked from 2005-01-03); BIL only from 2007-05-30, so BIL can only be an OOS sensitivity. ^IRX had missing days that were forward-filled (count in Gate 0).
- **2008 is the only deep bear in IS.** The seatbelt test's IS drawdown side rests mainly on one episode; OOS adds the fast 2020 crash and the 2022 stock-and-bond bear.
- **PEAD correlation is not measurable.** The repo holds trade-level PEAD results (2022 onward), not a daily return series over 2006 to now, so the diversification value against the live PEAD book is unknown.
- **Second round of tests on the same data.** v1 already tested B, its grid and two other strategies on exactly these prices and periods; v2 adds four configs, a grid and several sensitivities. Every additional test raises the chance that something passes by luck, and the OOS period has now been looked at twice — it is no longer a clean holdout for anything derived from v1's results.
- Sharpe uses rf = 0 (v1 convention). That favours cash-heavy series now that cash earns interest; the Sharpe ex T-bill columns and the re-evaluated tests show whether any verdict depends on it.
- BL charges costs per sub-account without netting; VT's 1.5x-cap variant borrows at the T-bill with no spread. Taxes and intra-month drift costs are not modelled. Fills at the open assume no slippage beyond 5 bp.
